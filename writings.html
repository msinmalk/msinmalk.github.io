  • Is John Wayne a knight of faith?

    I wonder, if John Wayne the Canadian, who shares a bed with 'The Moms' ( the Incandenza's not his own; Avril Incandenza Hal’s mother, the headmistress of ETA ), would be a Kierkegardian conventionally Abrahamic champ-of-life gets the princess where others don't surfs like silver using the power (power!) of the virtue of the absurd: Knight of Faith. Levels up from a knight of ''infinite'' resignation. Note the iconography in the title of Wayne's book ( DFW's I guess technically ) and the infinite in infinite resignation. It is in fact the same word. Now by chance? But these are two people who understood the depth of infinity. A tragic christian and a tragic hippie. It's why that description above sounds aloud at least somewhat gospelled and somewhat peace&love-like in one long breath. I hear it, don't worry that I don't. (We write in run-ons, here, both of the book writers in this little piece of work did, after all). It's just a job of implicating the importance of it despite all of that. Really. So John. Funny there exists a John Abraham out in the wild that just may be a candidate for another measurement- but alas Bollywood will wait. No, here it's John Wayne, and separately Abraham from the Holy Bible. The one with Abel & Cain. Shout out Steinbeck. A book of faith if you will. But Sam was resigned where Wayne (fingers crossed I'm not wrong) never was. Well. Did Wayne ever really wish for anything? That will be the most difficult establishment from the affirmative- whether John really ever had any motive. I guess for a knight of faith to be exposéd in a scene obviously enough to pin down as a correct application of the term from the outside looking in, it should be apparent to us this individual- because as far as I think I know the concept, a Knight of Faith is uniquely applied to those with the task of ' mentalizing '- both establishing their own mind and separately the minds of others- aka Individuals as I understand the term- has some ... quite literally simply 'want'. They want something. Don't at me with that contentment dialectic flyover- that we at once can be happy with what we have and understand that our efforts will positively affect our lives even still- it is a smaller task ours just to know that there would be something that makes them feel as though if God was good he would deliver it to them. Entitled I guess. Graeber says we owe an- again 'infinite' debt from being "cast into existence- existence existence existence". Now how about we feel we're owed instead? Yeah. KoF. (I'm not sure this really applies don't take me too seriously on this)- just the spirit of how that sense of deservedness survives all corrosion from beliefs in ones own (sinfulness)? Let's say John Wayne just didn't give a fuck. Which supposedly he didn't. When you say it out loud like that, though, you get a picture of a fellow behaving in spite rather than ... is obedience the right term? Am I satisfied describing the enactment of a command from the Holy up Above to this Abraham then, ahem, duly executed, as simple obedience? It's not really about the compliance though is it. It's about how to feel carrying the task out, and why the spitfeulness would be so ruinous to a candidacy for KoFhood (what we're shortening 'Knight of Faith'-hood down to). And down here maybe it's time to ask if Avril Incandenza (Hal’s mother, the headmistress of ETA), is the princess. That would be pretty cool for her. I digress. Digressingly I have to say that I am writing about princesses directly from the parallel of the ... first paradox? Movement? If you read it or look it (Fear & Trembling by Søren Kierkegaard) up it's just a merman and a princess as a parable for belief in the face of the ludacrisly un-seemingly-likely aka the power (power!) of the virtue of the absurd.
